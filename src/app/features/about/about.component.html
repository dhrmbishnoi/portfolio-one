About

I work between
design decisions
and engineering systems.

Seven years of building frontends for products that had to keep working after the launch post. The interesting problems are never in the first release — they are in the third year, when the team has changed and the requirements have moved twice.

I am a frontend engineer who spends most of the time on the parts of the work that users never see: the boundary between two features, the owner of a piece of state, the reason a component exposes the API it does.

That preference came from working on products that outlived their original architecture. When a codebase is still being changed two years later, the decisions that matter are the ones about ownership and boundaries — not the ones about which library to pick.

I work remotely, and I work best with a small product team where design and engineering are in the same conversation from the beginning. I would rather shape an interaction model with a designer than review a finished spec against what the framework can actually do.

The rest of this page is the set of positions I keep arriving back at. They are opinions, held loosely, revised often.
